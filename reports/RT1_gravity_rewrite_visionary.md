When c and ℏ are treated as derived quantities (not foundational), the gravity article opens three conceptual doors:

**Prediction:** In systems with engineered high coherence (S > 0.16), gravitational force increases by ~5-30%, depending on how much higher S rises toward 1. This is *directly testable* using precision torsion balances with coherent matter sources.

### Insight 3: The Equivalence Principle as Coherence Invariance

ODTOE naturally explains *why* gravitational mass equals inertial mass: both I(C) (configuration inertia) scale the same way with S and d. The equivalence principle is not a coincidence but a **topological theorem**: any reference frame is a choice of observation level d, and mass (measured gravitationally or inertially) measures resistance to reconfiguration at that level.
